One rule guides this manual: your writing stays on your computer.

What "local" means

When you use Ollama with a local model, the model runs on your processor — not on someone else's server. There is no account to create, no login, and no API key. Your prompts, drafts, unpublished manuscript, and personal letters never leave your machine.

Cloud AI vs. Ollama on your computer

Cloud AI toolsOllama on your computer
Your text is sent over the internetYour text never leaves your machine
You need an account, often a credit cardNo account, no payment
A company may store or review your inputOnly you can see what you type
You need internet to writeWorks offline after you download a model
The service can change or shut downThe model stays on your hard drive

What this guide does NOT do

  • Never asks you to paste an API key
  • Never asks you to create an account
  • Never sends your writing to a website
  • Never collects your email, name, or personal data

Practical privacy steps

  1. Download models from ollama.com only — the official library is the safest source.
  2. Work offline. Once a model is downloaded (ollama pull), you can turn off Wi-Fi and it still works.
  3. Keep drafts in files on your computer — any text editor or word processor.
  4. Delete models you no longer need: ollama rm <model-name>

A note on healthcare workers

If you are a nurse or other healthcare worker exploring AI, never paste real patient information into any AI tool, local or otherwise. Local AI keeps your words on your machine, but patient privacy rules still apply. This guide is for education only — never medical advice.

What Ollama itself can see

Ollama is installed on your computer, so it can see the models you run and the prompts you typed — the same way any program you install can see what you do with it. Ollama does not upload your prompts as part of normal operation. If you want to be extra cautious, disconnect from the internet after downloading your model.
